Losses caused by natural hazards have been increasing in recent decades, largely because of changes in the number and distribution of exposed assets, such as rapid urban development on land prone to geo-hazards. Knowing where buildings are and what they are like (the exposure) is needed to estimate expected losses and to prioritise actions during and after emergencies, but current exposure datasets rely on data collected only at long intervals, such as the ten-yearly building census.

SMILE explores how machine learning can produce up-to-date exposure layers by combining remote sensing images, ancillary data such as the national census, and data on buildings collected by trained citizens. The project also develops visualisation tools to present the resulting exposure to stakeholders and knowledge users.

The project is coordinated by the National Institute of Oceanography and Applied Geophysics (OGS), with CNR-IMATI Milano, the Department of Earth Sciences of the University of Florence and the Imaging and Vision Laboratory of the University of Milano-Bicocca. Our unit develops the machine learning methods used to analyse and classify image data, such as the classification of building attributes from street-view images.
